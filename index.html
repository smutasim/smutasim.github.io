About Me

I am a Senior Navigation Systems Engineer at The Aerospace Corporation, based in Colorado Springs. My work lives at the intersection of orbit determination, Kalman filtering, and positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT). Most days that means chasing down where something is, how confident I should be in that answer, and how to make the next estimate better than the last one.

I picked up a M.S. in Aerospace Engineering, focused on astrodynamics, from Purdue, and I am currently working through a M.S. in Computer Science at CU Boulder in whatever hours are left over. That second degree is less about switching careers and more about closing the gap between the orbital mechanics I already know cold and the machine learning tools that keep showing up in the same problems.

Outside of the day job I keep a running list of things I want to understand better, and every so often that list turns into a notebook, a script, or a small website like this one.

What I Work On

The short version: I help figure out where things are in space, and how sure we can be about it.

Orbit Determination Kalman Filtering PNT / GNSS EO / SAR Geolocation Applied Machine Learning Small-Body Astronomy

I have a handful of publications in ION and AIAA venues, mostly on the navigation and PNT side of that list. Lately I have also been spending time on small-body astronomy, using LSST data to help rank and classify asteroids.

Beyond the Office

Amateur Astronomy

I run my own observing sessions and have built tools to support them, including a planetary eyepiece planner with a full ephemeris and procedurally rendered planets. I am involved in LSST/SSSC working groups and attended the Sagan Summer Workshop, which was focused on the Roman Space Telescope and how microlensing and exoplanet transits will show up in its data.

Wildlife Photography

I photograph local Colorado wildlife, and would love to see a bear, bobcat, and red fox soon. I got tired of existing photo editors, so I built my own: a Python desktop app with its own Windows build script.

I also spend a fair amount of time on recreational dives into whatever has caught my attention that month: conservation, geoengineering, robotics, etc. Sometimes those dives turn into a prototype, like a small algorithm I built to test whether Kepler and TESS could be misclassifying transits caused by solar system small bodies occulting background stars rather than real exoplanets.
