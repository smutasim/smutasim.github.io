What are Keplerian Elements?

Keplerian elements describe an orbit as a fixed ellipse in space, plus where a body currently sits on that ellipse. The semi-major axis and eccentricity set the size and shape of the ellipse. Inclination, RAAN, and argument of periapsis orient that ellipse in three dimensions relative to the central body's equator and a reference direction. True anomaly is the one element that changes with time: it marks where the orbiting body is along the ellipse at a given instant.

What is ECI?

ECI, Earth-centered inertial, is a non-rotating frame centered on Earth (or, more generally, the central body) with axes fixed relative to distant stars rather than to the body's surface. It is the natural frame for propagating orbits, since Newton's laws apply directly in an inertial frame. The perifocal frame (PQW) is a convenient intermediate frame aligned with the orbit itself: P points toward periapsis, Q is 90 degrees ahead in the direction of motion, and W is along the orbit normal. A body-fixed frame, by contrast, rotates with the central body, which is what you want if you care about ground tracks or surface-relative geometry rather than inertial motion.

The Math

Position and velocity are first built in the perifocal frame directly from true anomaly, which avoids the branch and singularity issues that come from routing through eccentric anomaly:

p = a(1−e²), r = p / (1 + e·cosν), h = √(μp)
rPQW = [r·cosν, r·sinν, 0]
vPQW = (μ/h)·[−sinν, e+cosν, 0]

The perifocal state is then rotated into ECI with the standard 3-1-3 Euler rotation through Ω, i, and ω. For the rotating body-fixed frame, the ECI state is rotated once more by the body's rotation angle θ, and the velocity gets an extra −ω×r correction to account for the fact that the frame itself is spinning.
