Hohmann vs. Bi-Elliptic Transfer

Two ways to move between circular orbits, and the fuel bill for each one.

Watch the Difference

A Hohmann transfer uses a single transfer ellipse and two burns. A bi-elliptic transfer swings out past the final orbit first, on a much larger ellipse, then comes back down, for a total of three burns. The animation below shows both paths for the same start and end orbit.

The Math

For a transfer ellipse with periapsis and apoapsis radii rp and ra, the semi-major axis is a = (rp + ra)/2, and the speed at any radius r on that ellipse is v = √(μ(2/r − 1/a)), the vis-viva equation.

Hohmann: one ellipse, a = (r1+r2)/2, burns at r1 and r2.
Bi-elliptic: two ellipses through an intermediate radius rb, burns at r1, rb, and r2.

Total delta-v is the sum of the speed changes at each burn, and transfer time is half the orbital period of each ellipse used (Kepler's third law: T = 2π√(a³/μ)). The bi-elliptic path almost always takes longer, sometimes much longer, even in the cases where it costs less delta-v.
